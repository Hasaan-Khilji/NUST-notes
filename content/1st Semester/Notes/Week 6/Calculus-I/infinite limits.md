# Infinite Limits

Remember when we studied this limit?$$\lim_{x \to 0}f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0}\frac{1}{x}$$
We said that the limit is ==🔴undefined==, because when we approached 0 from the left, f(x) approached $- \infty$ and when we approached from the right, f(x) approached $+\infty$.
OK, fair enough, limits do *only exist* when right and left hand limits are the same.

But what if the right and left hand limits both approached infinity in the same direction? Like in $$\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{|x|}$$
Surely, the left and right hand limits are the same. But f(x) isn't really approaching a ***finite*** number, it's approaching ***infinity***. Can we say that the limit is **infinity**?
The answer is ==🟢**YES**==.

Moving away from [[asymptotes|asymptotes]], which were basically the behavior of functions as their independent variables approached infinity.
Infinite limits are the  when the actual values of the functions approach infinities (+ve or -ve) as their input variable approaches some number. So basically:

| $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = + \infty$ | $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = - \infty$ |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
