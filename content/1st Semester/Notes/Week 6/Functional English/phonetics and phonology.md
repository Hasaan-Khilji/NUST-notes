**Phonology** is the study of sounds of a language. Of how the language organizes the sounds to make words and convey meanings. As stated, phonology is limited to a specific language.

**Phonetics** is the study of sounds in general. Of how speakers produce sounds, how the sound waves travel and how listeners perceive them. Phonetics is a broader field of study as compared to phonology


| **Phonetics**                                                           | **Phonology**                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| General study of sounds in all languages are produced/perceived/travel. | Limited to the sounds of a single language    |
| Example: [p] is pronounced with closed lips                             | Example: /p/ and /b/ distinguish pat from bat |

# Branches of Phonetics

Phonetics has three branches:

1) Articulatory phonetics: what the speech organs do
2) Acoustic phonetics: measurable properties of sound waves
3) Auditory phonetics: how listeners hear and interpret sound


# Form airflow to speech

Air usually leaves from lungs and passes through the larynx.

The vocal folds may vibrate to produce a voiced sound; or not vibrate to produce an unvoiced sound.

The tongue, teeth, lips, palate and velum (soft palate) shape the airflow.

Nasal sounds send air through the nose.

# Places of Articulation

Sounds can be categorized into 11 categories on the basis of their place of articulation:

1) **Bilabial:** Produced by bringing both lips together.
	- /p/, /b/, /m/, /w/
2) **Labiodental:** Produced by placing the lower lip against the upper lip.
	- /f/, /v/
3) **Dental:** Produced by placing the tip of the tongue against or between the upper teeth.
	- /$\theta$/ as in theta, TH in THat
4) **Alveolar:** Produced by placing the tip of the tongue against the alveolar ridge.
	- /d/, /t/ 
5) **Post-alveolar:** Produced by placing the tip of the tongue just behind the alveolar ridge.
	- sh as in ship, ch as in chain, j as in jump
6) **Palatal:** Produced by raising the body of the tongue up towards  the hard palate.
	- /j/ as in yes
7) **Velar:** Produced by raising the back of the tongue towards the soft palate.
	- /k/, /g/, n as in ing
8) **Glottal:** Produced by constriction of the vocal fold.
	- /h/
