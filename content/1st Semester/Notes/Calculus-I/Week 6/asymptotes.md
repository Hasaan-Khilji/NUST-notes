# Asymptotes

An asymptote is a line that a function/curve approaches as it tends to infinity. Just as limits, asymptotes can never be fully reached/touched by the function/curve. A function/curve approaches the behavior of its asymptote.

Mathematically, an asymptote of the curve in the form $y = f(x)$ or $f(x,y)$ is a straight line such that the distance between the line and the function tends to zero as both approach infinity .

#### Which functions?

So, a common question is that: ***Which functions have asymptotes?***

The following type of functions always have asymptotes:
1) Exponential Functions
2) Logarithmic Functions
3) Trigonometric Functions (except for sinx and cosx)
4) Inverse Trigonometric Functions (except for arcsinx and arccosx)

These functions never have Asymptotes:
1) Simple Polynomial Functions
2) Absolute Value Functions
3) Basic Root Functions
4) sinx and cosx
5) arcsinx and arccosx

***Rational Functions*** can and cannot have asymptotes. If the degree of the denominator is 0, then it becomes a polynomial function. And if the numerator and denominator are the same, they'll cancel each other out and there'll be no asymptote.
Otherwise, rational functions always have asymptotes.
