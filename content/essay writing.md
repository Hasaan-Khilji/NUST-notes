An essay is a structured piece of writing that develops an idea, explains a subject, narrates an experience or argues a position.

#### Key Points of an Essay

When writing an essay, the following points should be taken under consideration:
- Purpose: inform, explain, reflect, narrate or persuade.
- Audience: the reader whose knowledge and expectations matters.
- Focus: one controlling idea or question.
- Development: readers, examples, facts, analysis, or details.

#### Common essay purposes

Following are four common essay purposes:

##### Narrative

A narrative essay tells/narrates a true or creative story or an experience, usually from a first person point of view.
For example: a day in the life of a dinosaur.

##### Descriptive

A descriptive essay creates a vivid impression of a person, place, object, or event using sensory details, figurative language, and the "show not tell" method to make the reader feel present in the scene.
For example: Some place I used to know.

##### Expository

An expository essay explains informs, or analyzes a subject using objective facts, reasoning and evidence without any personal opinions and emotional bias.
For example: The science of volcanoes

##### Argumentative

An argumentative essay makes a claim and defends it by collecting and providing evidence, presenting counter arguments, and rebutting them.
For example: Why Hassan Khilji should be the President

#### The Three Part Architecture

1) Introduction
	- The context, focus and thesis.

2) Body
	- Main points, evidence and analysis.

3) Conclusion
	- Synthesis and Final implication.

### The introduction

The introduction is the hook of any essay. If the hook is not compelling enough, the readers will be unmotivated to read it.
You have to get the readers' attention before the actual content of the essay. And you do that by writing a peak introduction.

Here are some tips to keep in mind when writing an introduction:

- Start with a relevant or compelling question or a bold claim.
- Narrow the focus of the topic so that the readers have an idea about what to expect from the essay. And by doing this, the topic becomes manageable for you to write too.
- If it's an expository or an argumentative essay, then the thesis should be stated clearly. Usually at the end of the introduction, so that it stands out.
- Avoid sweeping openings, dictionary referenced definitions and promises that the essay cannot fulfill.

#### The Thesis Statement

The thesis is the most important part of the introduction of an expository/argumentative essay. It's like a compass for these types of essays which guides the essay's entire flow and sometimes gives an outline for the essay's content. 
Since the thesis is your main claim, you can use it to keep your points in check; whether they're drifting too off topic or not. At any point while writing the essay, if you feel like what you're writing is going a bit off-route, you always refer back to the thesis.

Here are some qualities of a good thesis:
- It answers a question rather than merely naming the statement.
- It gives the essay a clear direction, for which it itself needs to be concise and focused, and not vague.
- It's key parts can become the main points.
